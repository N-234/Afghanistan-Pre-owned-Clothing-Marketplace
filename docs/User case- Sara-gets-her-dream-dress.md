# User Case: Sara Gets Her Dream Dress

**Project:** Afghan Girls’ Secondhand Clothes Marketplace  
**Product Owner:** Sara Qateh  
**Date:** October 13, 2026

## 1. Starting Situation

Sara is an Afghan university student who needs a beautiful dress for an upcoming university event. Since she has a limited budget, she prefers buying affordable secondhand clothes instead of expensive new ones.

To make secondhand shopping easier and more accessible for girls, Sara started the **Afghan Girls’ Secondhand Clothes Marketplace**, an online platform where girls can buy and sell used clothing.

Sara already has an account on the website and wants to find a dress that matches her style, size, and budget.

## 2. Normal Flow of Events

1. Sara logs in to her account on the website.
2. She visits the homepage and browses the available secondhand clothes.
3. She uses the search and filter options to find dresses that match her preferred size, style, and price range.
4. She finds a dress she likes and reviews its pictures, condition, size, price, and seller information.
5. She adds the dress to her favourites so she can compare it with other options.
6. After comparing several dresses, Sara chooses the one that best meets her needs.
7. She clicks the contact button to ask the seller whether the dress is still available.
8. The seller responds, and they agree on the price and the method of completing the purchase.
9. Sara is happy with her affordable choice and appreciates the opportunity to reuse clothing instead of buying something new.

## 3. Alternative Flows and Possible Problems

Several problems could occur while Sara is shopping. The website should handle them clearly and allow her to continue browsing.

| Possible Problem | Expected System Response |
|---|---|
| **Incorrect Password:** Sara enters the wrong password. | The website displays an error message and allows her to try again. |
| **Slow Internet Connection:** The website takes too long to load. | The website displays a loading indicator and provides a helpful message if the request fails. |
| **Dress Already Sold:** Sara finds a dress she likes, but it is no longer available. | The website displays its updated availability and suggests that Sara search for another dress. |
| **Insufficient Product Information:** The seller has not provided enough pictures or details. | Sara can contact the seller to request more information before making a decision. |
| **Seller Does Not Respond:** The seller does not reply to Sara's message. | Sara can contact another seller or continue browsing available dresses. |
| **Incorrect Product Details:** Sara discovers that the dress does not match its description. | She can decide not to purchase it and look for another suitable option. |

The website should be simple, user-friendly, and easy to navigate so Sara can continue shopping without unnecessary difficulties.

## 4. Other Concurrent Activities

While Sara searches for a dress, other users may also be using the marketplace.

- Sellers may add new clothes to the website.
- Sellers may update product descriptions, prices, sizes, and photographs.
- Sellers may mark items as sold or unavailable.
- Buyers may browse products, save dresses to their favourites, and contact sellers.
- Multiple users may search for the same dress at the same time.

The system must keep product and account information accurate and up to date. If a dress is sold, its availability should be updated so other buyers can avoid contacting the seller about an unavailable item.

The website should also handle simultaneous activities without losing changes or displaying outdated product information.

## 5. Final State

Sara successfully contacts the seller and agrees to purchase a dress within her budget.

At the end of the process:

- Sara has identified a dress that matches her preferences.
- She has received the necessary product information.
- She and the seller have agreed on the purchase details.
- The product's availability and account information remain accurate.
- Sara can continue searching if the dress is unavailable or the seller does not respond.

**Successful outcome:** Sara finds an affordable dress and has a positive shopping experience.

**Alternative outcome:** If the purchase cannot be completed, Sara can return to the marketplace and choose another dress.

## 6. Conclusion

The Afghan Girls’ Secondhand Clothes Marketplace helps Sara find affordable clothing that suits her style, size, and budget. This use case demonstrates the normal shopping process, possible errors, alternative scenarios, and the importance of keeping product information updated when multiple users access the website simultaneously.

By making secondhand clothing easier to find and purchase, the platform supports affordable fashion, encourages clothing reuse, and provides girls with a convenient way to buy and sell pre-owned clothes.
